# Every Puzzle Project

**Finding, transcribing and preserving every crossword ever printed.**

The first crossword appeared in 1913. Since then, countless newspapers,
magazines and books have printed them, and most survive only on paper or as
page scans that can be looked at but not searched. We're cataloguing those
publications, turning their puzzles into [xd](https://github.com/century-arcade/xdformat)
files (grid, clues, answers, author and date, each linked back to its source
scan), and checking every one by hand, so they can be searched, studied and
preserved for posterity.

Inspired by David Steinberg's [Pre-Shortzian Puzzle Project](http://www.preshortzianpuzzleproject.com/),
whose volunteers digitized New York Times crosswords from the pre-digital era.

This is a research archive, not a place to play old puzzles.

## News

**October 2026: our first full series.** Every crossword *Judge* magazine
printed from 1924 to 1939, 531 puzzles, has been read from archive.org scans into [xd](https://github.com/century-arcade/xdformat),
and checked by Claude and by people against the scans.
[How it went: what worked, what was hard, and what's next.](https://blitz.xwordapp.com/blog/judge.html)

## Where to start

- **[catalog](https://github.com/EveryPuzzleProject/catalog)**: every
  publication known to have printed a crossword. Report a puzzle you've seen
  or a publication we're missing; no technical skills needed.
- **[blitz](https://github.com/EveryPuzzleProject/blitz)**: help check the
  machine transcriptions against the original scans by directing Claude.
  Or check puzzles yourself on the [review site](https://blitz.xwordapp.com/review/).
